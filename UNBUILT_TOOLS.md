## 1. Tool 1: Air Pollution Tolerance Index & Anticipated Performance Index (APTI-API) Classifier

### 1.1. Scientific Context & Literature Foundation

A major challenge in urban greenbelt planning is selecting tree species that can act as active filters without succumbing to pollution-induced physiological stress. *Munam et al. (2025)* and *Parmar et al. (2016)* show that leaf dust capture capacity is not enough; if a species is highly sensitive, extreme particulate loading along busy transport corridors leads to rapid chlorophyll degradation, stomatal clogging, and tissue death.

To systemize species selection, the **Air Pollution Tolerance Index (APTI)** combines four critical biochemical parameters: ascorbic acid content, total chlorophyll, leaf extract pH, and relative water content. Ascorbic acid acts as a key antioxidant that scavenges reactive oxygen species (ROS) generated by heavy metal or soot deposition. Chlorophyll levels dictate photosynthetic health, pH stabilizes acidic gaseous uptake (like $SO_2$), and relative water content acts as a physiological buffer against transpiration stress.

To make this biochemically focused index useful for urban forestry, the **Anticipated Performance Index (API)** combines the APTI score with biological and socio-economic parameters (plant growth form/habit, canopy structure, foliage seasonality, and economic value) to rank tree species for roadside greenbelt implementation.

### 1.2. Mathematical Specification

* **Air Pollution Tolerance Index (APTI):**

  $$APTI = \frac{A \times (T + P) + R}{10}$$

  where:

  * $A$ is ascorbic acid content ($mg/g$ dry weight).
  * $T$ is total chlorophyll content ($mg/g$ dry weight).
  * $P$ is leaf extract pH (dimensionless, scale $1$ to $14$).
  * $R$ is relative water content of leaves ($\%$).

  APTI Threshold Classifications:

  * $APTI \ge 30$: Highly Tolerant (Apex Bio-filter)
  * $17 \le APTI < 30$: Tolerant
  * $11 \le APTI < 17$: Intermediate
  * $APTI < 11$: Sensitive (Avoid in high-exposure corridors)

* **Anticipated Performance Index (API):**

  Evaluates species on a $16$-point scale based on the following matrix:

  1. APTI Score (Max 8 points):
     * $APTI > 25$: $8$ points
     * $21 \le APTI \le 25$: $6$ points
     * $16 \le APTI < 21$: $4$ points
     * $10 \le APTI < 16$: $2$ points
     * $APTI < 10$: $0$ points
  2. Growth Habit & Canopy Structure (Max 4 points):
     * Dense, spreading tree canopy (e.g., *Ficus religiosa*): $4$ points
     * Open, vertical tree canopy (e.g., *Polyalthia longifolia*): $2$ points
     * Shrub or lower-understory growth form: $1$ point
  3. Foliage Seasonality (Max 2 points):
     * Evergreen (continuous year-round bio-filtration): $2$ points
     * Deciduous (seasonal filtration gap): $1$ point
  4. Economic & Urban Utility (Max 2 points):
     * High economic/aesthetic/shade value: $2$ points
     * Moderate economic value: $1$ point
     * Low utility: $0$ points

  API Suitability Grading:

  * $15 \le API \le 16$: Excellent (Apex Greenbelt Choice - 5 Stars)
  * $13 \le API \le 14$: Very Good (Highly Recommended - 4 Stars)
  * $10 \le API \le 12$: Good (Recommended - 3 Stars)
  * $6 \le API \le 9$: Fair (Conditional Selection - 2 Stars)
  * $API < 6$: Poor (Not Recommended - 1 Star)

## 2. Tool 2: Heavy Metal Translocation & Dietary Exposure Modeler (HM-DETM)

### 2.1. Scientific Context & Literature Foundation

Vascular plants actively translocate soil contaminants upward into their aerial tissues. *Moțiu et al. (2026)* analyze how urban street trees absorb heavy metals (such as Lead, Cadmium, Chromium, and Nickel) from polluted soils and deposit them in leaves and flowers. Critically, these contaminated leaves are frequently harvested to prepare herbal infusions and teas (such as linden tea from *Tilia spp.*).

At the same time, *Moniuszko et al. (2026)* demonstrate that heavy metals on foliar surfaces enter terrestrial food webs, suppressing defoliator eclosion and survival.

Despite these hazards, there is no standardized dietary risk engine to model:

1. How easily a species translocates heavy metals from soil to leaves.
2. The rate at which these metals dissolve into water during brewing.
3. The resulting chronic toxicological risk to human consumers (expressed as the Daily Intake of Metal and Hazard Quotient).

### 2.2. Mathematical Specification

* **Soil-to-Leaf Translocation Factor ($TF$):**

  $$TF = \frac{C_{leaf}}{C_{soil}}$$

  where:

  * $C_{leaf}$ is concentration of heavy metal in leaf dry matter ($mg/kg$).
  * $C_{soil}$ is concentration of heavy metal in localized surface soil ($mg/kg$).
  * $TF > 1.0$ indicates a hyperaccumulating plant that actively concentrates metals in foliar tissue.

* **Daily Intake of Metal ($DIM$, $mg/kg \cdot day^{-1}$):**

  $$DIM = \frac{C_{leaf} \times W_{leaf} \times \eta \times V_{intake}}{10^5 \times V_{water} \times BW}$$

  where:

  * $C_{leaf}$ is leaf heavy metal concentration ($mg/kg$).
  * $W_{leaf}$ is dry leaf/flower mass used for steeping ($g$).
  * $\eta$ is water extraction/transfer rate ($0.0\%$ to $100.0\%$), representing how easily the metal dissolves during brewing (e.g., Chrome/Nickel extraction $>80\%$, Copper/Zinc $<10\%$).
  * $V_{intake}$ is daily volume of the tea consumed ($L$).
  * $V_{water}$ is total water volume used to prepare the steep ($L$).
  * $BW$ is body weight of the consumer ($kg$).
  * $10^5$ is an aggregated conversion constant.

* **Hazard Quotient ($HQ$):**

  $$HQ = \frac{DIM}{RfD}$$

  where:

  * $RfD$ is Oral Reference Dose ($mg/kg \cdot day^{-1}$) established by the US EPA:
    * Lead (Pb): $RfD = 0.0035$ (standard toxicological proxy limit)
    * Cadmium (Cd): $RfD = 0.0005$
    * Chromium (Cr): $RfD = 0.0030$
    * Nickel (Ni): $RfD = 0.0200$
    * Copper (Cu): $RfD = 0.0400$
    * Zinc (Zn): $RfD = 0.3000$

  Toxicological Alerts:

  * $HQ \ge 1.0$: Toxic Ingestion Hazard (potential chronic toxicity)
  * $0.2 \le HQ < 1.0$: Elevated Risk (requires monitoring)
  * $HQ < 0.2$: Safe

## 3. Tool 3: Canopy Deposition Velocity & Plume Mitigation Simulator (CDVPMS)

### 3.1. Scientific Context & Literature Foundation

Municipal green infrastructure is frequently designed as a physical barrier to block vehicle emissions. *Bealey et al. (2007)* and *Nowak et al. (2006)* deploy large-scale GIS models (such as UFORE) to compute nationwide particulate dry deposition. These models rely on the concept of **Deposition Velocity ($V_d$)**, which represents the rate at which suspended particles settle onto leaves.

To bring this down to a practical design scale, the **Canopy Deposition Velocity & Plume Mitigation Simulator (CDVPMS)** models:

1. How wind speed, leaf pubescence (trichome density), and Leaf Area Index (LAI) scale $V_d$ for specific particle sizes ($PM_{10}$, $PM_{2.5}$, $PM_{0.2}$).
2. The absolute mass of PM (in grams) captured by a greenbelt canopy over time.
3. The downwind concentration reduction, modeling how a greenbelt scrubs a passing pollution plume.

### 3.2. Mathematical Specification

* **Dry Deposition Velocity ($V_d$, $cm/s$):**

  $$V_d = V_{d,base} \times LAI \times \left(\frac{u}{u_0}\right) \times (1 + P_{factor})$$

  where:

  * $V_{d,base}$ is base deposition velocity at $u_0 = 2.0\text{ m/s}$ and $LAI = 1.0$:
    * $PM_{10}$ (coarse particles): $V_{d,base} = 0.64\text{ cm/s}$
    * $PM_{2.5}$ (fine particles): $V_{d,base} = 0.16\text{ cm/s}$
    * $PM_{0.2}$ (ultrafine particles): $V_{d,base} = 0.04\text{ cm/s}$
  * $LAI$ is Leaf Area Index ($m^2$ leaf area / $m^2$ ground area).
  * $u$ is local wind speed ($m/s$).
  * $P_{factor}$ is pubescence multiplier ($0.45$ if leaves are hairy/pubescent, $0$ if smooth) to model trichome interception.

  Physical Capping Limits: Capped to prevent physically impossible velocities (PM10 capped at $6.0\text{ cm/s}$, PM2.5 at $2.5\text{ cm/s}$, PM0.2 at $1.0\text{ cm/s}$).

* **Particulate Deposition Flux ($F$, $\mu g/m^2 \cdot s$):**

  $$F = V_d \times C \times 10^{-2}$$

  where:

  * $C$ is ambient atmospheric PM concentration ($\mu g/m^3$).
  * $10^{-2}$ is standard conversion constant ($m$ to $cm$).

* **Total PM Mass Removed ($M$, grams):**

  $$M = F \times A_{canopy} \times t \times 10^{-6}$$

  where:

  * $A_{canopy}$ is land area covered by the greenbelt canopy ($m^2$).
  * $t$ is exposure duration in seconds ($t = \text{Hours} \times 3600$).
  * $10^{-6}$ is conversion constant ($\mu g$ to grams).

* **Downwind Plume Scrubbing Concentration ($C_{downwind}$, $\mu g/m^3$):**

  $$C_{downwind} = C \times e^{-\frac{V_{d,m/s} \times LAI \times W}{H \times u}}$$

  where:

  * $V_{d,m/s}$ is deposition velocity converted to $m/s$ ($V_d \times 10^{-2}$).
  * $W$ is width of the greenbelt barrier along the wind vector ($m$).
  * $H$ is local atmospheric mixing height above the canopy (standardized at $10\text{ m}$).
  * $u$ is wind speed ($m/s$).

  Removal Efficiency ($\%$): $\frac{C - C_{downwind}}{C} \times 100$.

## 4. Cross-Industry Computational Tools

To expand the utility of these biomonitoring models across multiple sectors, five additional mathematical tools have been built. These extend foliar dynamics to occupational health, crop protection, HVAC design, brownfield redevelopment, and sustainable building energy.

### 4.1. Tool 4: Skin Dermal Partition & Exposure Modeler (SDPEM)

* **Code Implementation:** [DermalPartitionSimulator](file:///d:/PROJECT/ddos/models.js)
* **Target Industries:** Occupational Health & Safety, Chemical Manufacturing, Industrial Hygiene.
* **Scientific Context:** Plant leaves capture airborne semi-volatile organic pollutants (like PAHs) through cuticular wax partition coefficients. Similarly, human skin surface lipids partition organic contaminants from ambient air. This tool models human skin absorption rates for workers in contaminated air environments, applying the same organic partitioning models.
* **Mathematical Formulation:**

  * Skin-Air Partition Coefficient ($K_{skin\_air}$):
    $$K_{skin\_air} = 10^{0.7 \log K_{ow} - 1.5}$$
    where $K_{ow}$ is the octanol-water partition coefficient of the organic contaminant.

  * Max Skin Equilibrium Capacity ($M_{max}$, $\mu g$):
    $$M_{max} = C_{air} \times V_{lipid} \times K_{skin\_air}$$
    where $C_{air}$ is ambient air concentration ($\mu g/m^3$) and $V_{lipid}$ is the skin lipid volume ($m^3$, converted from $mL$).

  * Dermal Dose Absorbed ($M_{absorbed}$, $\mu g$):
    $$M_{absorbed} = M_{max} \times (1 - e^{-k_{absorb} \times t})$$
    where $k_{absorb}$ is the physiological absorption rate ($hour^{-1}$) and $t$ is exposure time in hours.

### 4.2. Tool 5: Agricultural Pesticide Spray Retention & Washoff Modeler (APSRWM)

* **Code Implementation:** [PesticideWashoffModeler](file:///d:/PROJECT/ddos/models.js)
* **Target Industries:** Agro-chemicals, Precision Agriculture, Environmental Protection.
* **Scientific Context:** In farming, crop sprays are lost when rainfall washes pesticides off leaves and into the soil. This tool adapts rainfall wash-off kinetics from plant biomonitoring to compute pesticide canopy retention rates. It scales by crop wax types and models rain-fastening adjuvants to optimize agricultural efficiency and reduce runoff contamination.
* **Mathematical Formulation:**

  * Intercepted Foliar Mass ($M_{foliar}$, $mg$):
    $$M_{foliar} = C_{spray} \times V_{spray} \times Adhesion_{crop}$$
    where $C_{spray}$ is spray active ingredient concentration ($mg/L$), $V_{spray}$ is applied volume ($L$), and $Adhesion_{crop}$ is the crop-specific leaf adhesion coefficient ($0.0$ to $1.0$).

  * Wash-off Fraction ($F_{washoff}$):
    $$F_{washoff} = 1 - e^{-\beta_{wash} \times Rainfall \times Adjuvant_{factor}}$$
    where $\beta_{wash}$ is the crop wash-off rate constant ($mm^{-1}$), $Rainfall$ is the rain volume ($mm$), and $Adjuvant_{factor}$ scales from $1.0$ (no adjuvant) down to $0.25$ (rain-fast stickers).

  * Leached to Soil ($M_{leached}$, $mg$):
    $$M_{leached} = M_{foliar} \times F_{washoff}$$

  * Retained on Crop ($M_{retained}$, $mg$):
    $$M_{retained} = M_{foliar} \times (1 - F_{washoff})$$

### 4.3. Tool 6: HVAC Filter Clogging & Fan Power Energy Predictor (HFCFPEP)

* **Code Implementation:** [FilterCloggingEnergyPredictor](file:///d:/PROJECT/ddos/models.js)
* **Target Industries:** HVAC Engineering, Building Energy Management, Indoor Air Quality (IAQ).
* **Scientific Context:** Atmospheric dust deposits on leaves, clogging stomatal pores and reducing plant transpiration. In building air systems, particulates build up on HVAC filters (MERV/HEPA), creating a dust cake. This tool models the pressure drop across clogged filters, calculating the increase in fan power draw and the resulting electricity energy overhead.
* **Mathematical Formulation:**

  * Cumulative Filter Dust Cake ($M_{dust}$, $mg$):
    $$M_{dust} = C_{PM} \times Q_{flow} \times t \times 10^{-3}$$
    where $C_{PM}$ is ambient PM concentration ($\mu g/m^3$), $Q_{flow}$ is HVAC airflow ($m^3/h$), and $t$ is runtime in hours.

  * Filter Clogging Fraction ($C_{clog}$):
    $$C_{clog} = 1 - e^{-\delta \times M_{dust} \times 10^{-5}}$$
    where $\delta$ is the dust cake compaction factor ($mg^{-1}$).

  * Clogged Pressure Drop ($dP_{clogged}$, $Pa$):
    $$dP_{clogged} = dP_{clean} \times (1 + 8 \times C_{clog}^2)$$
    where $dP_{clean}$ is the clean filter pressure drop ($Pa$).

  * Fan Power Increase ($\Delta W$, $Watts$):
    $$\Delta W = W_{clean} \times \left(\frac{dP_{clogged}}{dP_{clean}} - 1\right)$$
    where $W_{clean}$ is the clean fan operating power ($Watts$).

  * Excess Energy Overhead ($E_{overhead}$, $kWh$):
    $$E_{overhead} = \frac{\Delta W \times t}{1000}$$

### 4.4. Tool 7: Soil Heavy Metal Phytoremediation Sizing & ROI Estimator (SHMPS)

* **Code Implementation:** [PhytoCleanupModeler](file:///d:/PROJECT/ddos/models.js)
* **Target Industries:** Civil Engineering, Real Estate Development, Land Remediation, Soil Science.
* **Scientific Context:** Industrial sites are often contaminated with heavy metals (Lead, Cadmium, Zinc). Digging and dumping contaminated soil in landfills is expensive. Phytoremediation using hyperaccumulators is a low-cost, green alternative. This tool models the multi-year cleanup timeline of phytoremediation using plant bioconcentration factors and compares the total cost against excavation to compute financial return on investment (ROI).
* **Mathematical Formulation:**

  * Total Soil Mass ($M_{soil}$, $kg$):
    $$M_{soil} = Area_{site} \times depth_{soil} \times \rho_{soil}$$
    where $Area_{site}$ is the site size ($m^2$), $depth_{soil}$ is soil depth ($m$), and $\rho_{soil}$ is soil bulk density (standardized at $1300\text{ kg/m}^3$).

  * Metal Extraction Fraction per Cycle ($F_{extract}$):
    $$F_{extract} = \min\left(0.95, \frac{Biomass_{ha} \times Area_{ha} \times BCF}{M_{soil}}\right)$$
    where $Biomass_{ha}$ is crop dry biomass yield per hectare ($kg/ha$), $Area_{ha}$ is site area in hectares, and $BCF$ is the plant-to-soil bioconcentration factor.

  * Remediation Cycles/Years Required ($N$):
    $$N = \left\lceil \frac{\ln(C_{target} / C_{init})}{\ln(1 - F_{extract})} \right\rceil$$
    where $C_{init}$ is initial soil metal concentration ($mg/kg$) and $C_{target}$ is regulatory safety threshold ($mg/kg$).

  * Total Phyto Cost ($Cost_{phyto}$, $\$$):
    $$Cost_{phyto} = Area_{ha} \times Cost_{cycle} \times N$$
    where $Cost_{cycle}$ is planting and disposal cost per hectare per cycle.

  * Excavation Cost ($Cost_{excav}$, $\$$):
    $$Cost_{excav} = \left(\frac{M_{soil}}{1000}\right) \times Cost_{ton}$$
    where $Cost_{ton}$ is excavation, transport, disposal, and backfill cost (standardized at $\$140/\text{ton}$).

  * Financial Savings:
    $$\text{Savings} = Cost_{excav} - Cost_{phyto}$$
    $$\text{Savings Percent} = \frac{\text{Savings}}{Cost_{excav}} \times 100$$

### 4.5. Tool 8: Urban Green Wall Evapotranspirational Cooling & Building HVAC Offset Calculator (GWECB)

* **Code Implementation:** [GreenWallThermalPredictor](file:///d:/PROJECT/ddos/models.js)
* **Target Industries:** Sustainable Architecture, Urban Planning, Smart Cities, Building HVAC Design.
* **Scientific Context:** Vertical green walls provide microclimatic cooling through plant transpiration and reduce building solar heat gains by shading exterior walls. This tool computes the daily volume of water transpired, the corresponding cooling energy offset, the reduction in HVAC electricity consumption, electricity bill savings, and carbon emissions reduction.
* **Mathematical Formulation:**

  * Daily Transpired Water Volume ($V_{transp}$, $L$):
    $$V_{transp} = Area_{wall} \times LAI \times Rate_{transp}$$
    where $Area_{wall}$ is the green wall surface area ($m^2$), $LAI$ is Leaf Area Index, and $Rate_{transp}$ is the transpiration rate per unit leaf area ($L/m^2\cdot day$).

  * Latent Cooling Energy Offset ($E_{latent}$, $kWh$):
    $$E_{latent} = V_{transp} \times \lambda_{vap} \times 0.277778$$
    where $\lambda_{vap} = 2.45\text{ MJ/L}$ is the latent heat of vaporization of water, and $0.277778$ converts $MJ$ to $kWh$.

  * Shading Cooling Energy Offset ($E_{shading}$, $kWh$):
    $$E_{shading} = Area_{wall} \times BaseSolarLoad \times \left(\frac{Reduction\%}{100}\right)$$
    where $BaseSolarLoad$ is direct solar heat load (standardized at $4.0\text{ kWh/m}^2\cdot day$) and $Reduction\%$ is the percentage of solar heat blocked by vegetation.

  * Building HVAC Electricity Savings ($E_{saved}$, $kWh$):
    $$E_{saved} = \frac{E_{latent} + E_{shading}}{COP}$$
    where $COP$ is the Coefficient of Performance of the HVAC cooling system.

  * Monetary and Carbon Offsets:
    $$\text{Daily Financial Savings} = E_{saved} \times Cost_{electricity}$$
    $$\text{Daily CO2 Mitigated (kg)} = E_{saved} \times 0.38$$
    where $0.38\text{ kg CO2/kWh}$ is the standard electrical power emission factor.

## 5. Verification and Automated Testing

These tools have been fully verified under [test_models.js](file:///d:/PROJECT/ddos/test_models.js). The assertion suite verifies the following edge cases:

* **APTI Calculation Validation:** Verifies that a sensitive biochemical profile returns an index of $8.40$ (Sensitive), while a high-antioxidant, high-water profile returns $32.50$ (Highly Tolerant).
* **API Decision Tree:** Confirms that a dense, evergreen canopy with high tolerance scores yields an API of $16/16$ (5 Stars, Excellent), whereas a deciduous shrub with poor tolerance scores yields $2/16$ (1 Star, Poor).
* **HM-DETM Mass-Balance and Risk Assertions:**
  * Confirms a safe cup of tea ($C_{leaf} = 0.5\text{ mg/kg}$ Lead) yields $HQ = 0.002$ (Safe).
  * Confirms a toxic cup ($C_{leaf} = 500\text{ mg/kg}$ Lead consumed by a child) yields an extreme $HQ = 214.28$ (Toxic Ingestion Hazard warning).
* **Canopy Deposition Wind and Size Scaling:**
  * Verifies that PM10 deposition velocity ($1.28\text{ cm/s}$) is scaled appropriately compared to PM0.2 ($0.08\text{ cm/s}$).
  * Assures that a $5000\text{ m}^2$ greenbelt exposed to $100\text{ }\mu g/m^3$ of PM2.5 over $10\text{ hours}$ removes exactly $115.20\text{ grams}$ of particulates, scrubbing downwind concentration by $3.77\%$.
* **Dermal Partition Inhalation vs Skin Absorption:**
  * Confirms that a safe VOC profile (Toluene) yields a very low partitioned dose ($0.0002\text{ }\mu g$), maintaining safe status.
  * Confirms that high Benzo[a]pyrene exposure ($8000\text{ }\mu g/m^3$) leads to partitioned skin loading of $24.72\text{ }\mu g$ and dermal absorption of $5.27\text{ }\mu g$, triggering the `CRITICAL SKIN UPTAKE HAZARD` warning.
* **Pesticide Washoff Crop and Adjuvant Interactions:**
  * Verifies that spraying a broadleaf crop with $400\text{ mg}$ of pesticide leads to an initial foliar load of $280\text{ mg}$.
  * Confirms that a $10\text{ mm}$ rainfall wash-off flushes $154.2\text{ mg}$ to the soil, triggering the `CRITICAL ECO-TOXIC SOIL RUNOFF` safety flag.
* **HVAC Filter Clogging Power Penalties:**
  * Verifies that a MERV 13 filter running for $10\text{ days}$ in $100\text{ }\mu g/m^3$ of ambient PM captures $48.0\text{ grams}$ of dust, clogging the filter by $51.3\%$.
  * Asserts that pressure drop spikes to $372.9\text{ Pa}$ and fan energy overhead rises by $303.5\text{ kWh}$, triggering the `CRITICAL PRESSURE RESISTANCE / REPLACE FILTER` system warning.
* **Phytoremediation Sizing & Financial Timeline Analysis:**
  * Verifies that cleaning a site of $5000\text{ m}^2$ contaminated at $150\text{ mg/kg}$ down to $15\text{ mg/kg}$ using a standard low-yield plant takes $374$ cycles, costing $\$374,000$, and is flagged as `PHYTOREMEDIATION NOT RECOMMENDED / TIMELINE TOO LONG`.
  * Verifies that a highly hyperaccumulating plant can clean a $2000\text{ m}^2$ site in $1$ cycle, costing only $\$600$ and saving $\$54,000$ ($98.9\%$), triggering `PHYTOREMEDIATION HIGHLY FEASIBLE`.
* **Green Wall Evapotranspiration Savings:**
  * Verifies that a $150\text{ m}^2$ green wall with a leaf area index of $4$ transpires $1500\text{ L}$ of water per day.
  * Confirms that latent heat vapor cooling saves $1020.83\text{ kWh}$ and wall shading saves $210\text{ kWh}$, for a total thermal savings of $1230.83\text{ kWh}$ ($384.64\text{ kWh}$ of electrical energy saved, or $\$57.70$ and $146.16\text{ kg CO2}$ daily reduction), categorized as `EXCEPTIONAL COOLING OFFSET` under `APEX ENERGY SAVING INFRASTRUCTURE`.
